IT MODULE 1 Cybersecurity Operating Model & SEBI CSCRF Architecture, Clocks & Governance
Oct 9, 2026 Session · Deepankar Chatterjee (GM-IT, SEBI) · Slides 1–12 & 36

Directly synthesized from NISM-2.pptx (Cybersecurity operations in Capital Markets, Oct 09, 2026) inside the oct 9 subfolder.

1.1 The Cybersecurity Operating Model (Slides 1–2)

  • Core Definition (Slide 1): Cybersecurity is not merely a control catalogue or a technical team; it is the operating discipline that connects daily signals to assurance, decisions, and accepted risk.
  • The 5-Stage Clockwise Operating Loop (Slide 2):
    1. Know: Assets, threats, and obligations (Exam Q: Which stage becomes weakest first when an asset inventory is wrong? → Know, which then cascades across all subsequent stages).
    2. Protect: Controls and hardening to reduce exposure.
    3. Detect: Turns telemetry into signals and anomalies.
    4. Respond: Contain harm and recover / restore service.
    5. Learn: Evidence and improvement — with a mandatory feedback arrow Learn ↶ Know (learning feeds the next view of what matters; "a control that never improves becomes ceremony").
  • Where the SOC Sits: The Security Operations Center (SOC) sits mainly in Detect and Respond, but depends on every stage of the loop.

1.2 SEBI CSCRF Scope, 5 RE Tiers, 6 Functions & 5 Resilience Goals (Slides 3–7)

SEBI CSCRF Circular: SEBI/HO/ITD-1/ITD_CSC_EXT/P/CIR/2024/113, issued August 20, 2024.
• Purpose: Consolidate and replace older fragmented 2015 / 2018 cyber guidelines.
• Coverage: Covers 22+ categories of SEBI-regulated entities (brokers, AMCs, depositories, merchant bankers, RTAs, etc.).
• Rollout: Phased implementation through 2025, with non-MII compliance deadlines extending to August 31, 2025.
  • Five Tiers Scaling Obligations to Market Impact (Slide 5 — "Correct tier mapping is the first compliance decision"):
    1. MIIs (Market Infrastructure Institutions): Stock exchanges, clearing corporations, and depositories.
    2. Qualified REs (QREs): Large or high-impact intermediaries.
    3. Mid-size REs: Moderate scale and AUM.
    4. Small-size REs: Lower AUM and client-base thresholds.
    5. Self-certification REs: Smallest entities with baseline duties.
  • Six Cybersecurity Functions — Built on NIST CSF 2.0 Function Model (Slide 6):
    1. 1. Govern (GV): Board accountability, policies, and CISO oversight.
    2. 2. Identify (ID): Asset discovery and critical-system mapping.
    3. 3. Protect (PR): Access controls, encryption, and zero-trust architecture.
    4. 4. Detect (DE): Continuous monitoring and Market SOC (M-SOC) integration.
    5. 5. Respond (RS): Incident management and Cyber Crisis Management Plans (CCMP).
    6. 6. Recover (RC) + Evolve (EV): Disaster recovery and continuous posture improvement.
  • Five Cyber Resilience Goals (Slide 7):
    1. 1. Anticipate: Predict and pre-empt emerging threats.
    2. 2. Withstand: Maintain core operations during attack.
    3. 3. Contain: Limit lateral movement and blast radius.
    4. 4. Recover: Restore critical services within defined windows.
    5. 5. Evolve: Improve defenses using post-incident learning.

1.4 CISO Governance, Supply Chain, August 2025 Clarifications & ISSC (Slides 11, 12 & 36)

  • CISO Accountability at the Top (Slide 11):
    • Top-tier entities must appoint a dedicated, full-time CISO.
    • The CISO reports to the MD/CEO or Board, with organizational standing equivalent to the CTO/CIO.
    • Part-time CISOs are banned; shared or remote CISO arrangements face strict restrictions.
  • Outward Supply Chain Controls (Slide 11): Vendors require background checks, Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs), and third-party risk assessments.
  • Four Key Pillars of the August 2025 CSCRF Clarifications (Slide 12):
    1. Multi-regulator principle: Apply exclusivity and equivalence for entities regulated under multiple watchdogs.
    2. Zero trust: Move from legacy perimeter security toward network segmentation and IT-committee oversight.
    3. Audit alignment: Use CERT-In-aligned methodologies for empanelled auditing agencies.
    4. Threshold updates: Revisit AUM and activity thresholds for merchant bankers and portfolio managers.
  • Information Security Steering Committee — ISSC (Slide 36 Speaker Notes):
    • Nature & Chair: Management-level committee, typically chaired by a senior executive, overseeing information security.
    • Composition: CISO, IT head, business unit heads, risk, compliance, and internal audit representatives.
    • Meeting Frequency: Meets at defined intervals, usually quarterly or more often, with minuted decisions and action tracking.
    • Core Functions: Approve/review security policies; review posture, incident trends & SOC metrics; prioritise security investments and resolve business-vs-control conflicts; monitor closure of audit & VAPT observations; review regulatory compliance; and escalate material matters to the Board or Risk Management Committee.

IT MODULE 3 SEBI Cloud Governance Framework, Sovereignty & Contractual Controls
Oct 9, 2026 Session · Deepankar Chatterjee (GM-IT, SEBI) · Slides 16–25

3.1 Cloud Accountability, Regulatory Arc & 4-Step Control Starting Point (Slides 16–19)

  • Core Principle (Slide 16–17): "Cloud is outsourced. Accountability is not." The question is no longer only where workloads run, but whether a regulated entity can remain accountable, evidenced, and operational when the cloud or a supplier fails.
  • Shared Responsibility Matrix (Slide 17):
    • 1. CSP Controls (Service Environment): Infrastructure, platform safeguards, availability commitments, and provider-operated security controls.
    • 2. RE-Owned Controls (Regulated Entity): Confidentiality, integrity, availability, security, compliance, and the decision to accept risk.
  • Three-Step Regulatory Arc (Slide 18):
    1. 06 March 2023: Framework for Adoption of Cloud Services (Circular SEBI/HO/ITD/ITD_VAPT/P/CIR/2023/033).
    2. 20 August 2024: Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF) (Circular SEBI/HO/ITD-1/ITD_CSC_EXT/P/CIR/2024/113).
    3. 31 December 2024 → 2025: CSCRF clarifications, FAQs, and technical clarifications turning expectations into operating detail.
  • 03 · Control Starting Point (Slide 19 — "The inventory is the bridge between business services and control ownership"):
    1. 01 Inventory: Map assets, applications, data, cryptographic assets, and dependencies.
    2. 02 Classify: Classify critical and non-critical systems and understand their failure impact.
    3. 03 Apply controls: Apply controls proportionate to impact.
    4. 04 Multiple licenses: For an entity holding multiple SEBI licenses, use the highest applicable CSCRF category.

3.2 Data Sovereignty Design, 4 Contractual Control Surfaces & Operating Evidence (Slides 20–25)

  • 04 · Data Design — Data Sovereignty & Encryption Constraints (Slide 20):
    • Choose the provider: Use only MeitY-empanelled Cloud Service Providers (CSPs) with valid STQC-certified data centers.
    • Keep data and keys in India: Keep storage and processing in certified Indian data centers; keep encryption-key management within India.
    • Test the model: Assess BYOK (Bring Your Own Key) and encryption at rest, in motion, and in use (including for SaaS services).
Contract Surface (Slide 21) What to Make Explicit in the Cloud Contract Required Evidence or Remedy
1. Service operation Cyber requirements in SLAs, patching, logs, monitoring, and incident duties Service reports, control evidence, escalation path
2. Supplier chain Audit material subcontractors whose failure could affect CSP delivery Assessment rights, findings, remediation tracking
3. Regulatory access Audit rights, SEBI regulatory access, source code or escrow where relevant Access clause, retained records, tested retrieval
4. Exit and recovery Exit plans, data return, continuity, and responsibility boundaries Exit test, recovery evidence, accountable owner
  • 06 · Operating Evidence (Slide 22 — "Maturity is visible when operating activity, remediation, and proof line up"):
    1. 1. Operate: VAPT, patch management, secure testing, logging, monitoring, and threat intelligence.
    2. 2. Remediate: Close vulnerabilities within stated timelines and test patches before production and DR deployment.
    3. 3. Demonstrate: Cyber audits, Cyber Capability Index (CCI) evidence, SBOMs (Software Bill of Materials), and SOC / M-SOC integration.
  • 07 · Failure Mode when Cloud or Supplier Fails (Slide 23): 01 Trigger (CSP disruption, lost connectivity, compromised credentials, data corruption) → 02 Respond (Incident response & forensic readiness preserve decisions, facts, and evidence) → 03 Recover (Backup, recovery, and DC-DR drills restore the regulated service) → 04 Prove (Contractual recovery objectives, escalation paths, communications, and evidence retention).
  • 08 · Practical Implementation Roadmap (Slide 24): Baseline → Harden → Prove → Improve.
  • Three Board Questions (Slide 25 — "Can we operate safely through disruption?"): (1) Which regulated services depend on cloud or material subcontractors? (2) Can we prove where sensitive data, keys, logs, and backups reside? (3) How quickly can we detect, contain, recover, and evidence a cyber event?

    • All 7 Official Terms of Reference (ToR) of HPSC-CS:
      1. To oversee and provide overall guidance on cyber security initiatives for SEBI and for the entire capital market.
      2. To advise SEBI in developing and maintaining cyber security & cyber resilience requirements aligned with global best practices and industry standards in accordance with Indian capital market structure.
      3. To identify measures to improve cyber resilience and related business continuity and disaster recovery (BCP/DR) processes in the Indian securities market.
      4. To provide recommendations for strengthening processes to audit cyber security & cyber resilience setups in the Indian securities market.
      5. To periodically review the mandate and functioning of SOCs (Security Operations Centers) and to guide SEBI in setting up a Cyber Lab / Cyber Center of Excellence for the securities market.
      6. To study major cyber-attack incidents related to financial markets in domestic and global markets and identify gaps in the existing framework.
      7. To engage in continuous dialogues with external agencies: CERT-In, National Cyber Coordination Centre (NCSC) / National Security Council Secretariat (NSCS), Department of Telecommunications (DoT), Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), and leading academic institutions/organizations.

5.1 Global & Indian Evolution of Capital Markets & MII Core Plumbing

  • Global Origins (Slides 5–10):
    • Osaka's Dojima Rice Exchange (Japan): Early standardization and evolution of futures markets.
    • 1602 — Dutch East India Company (VOC) & Amsterdam: Pooling permanent capital from many investors with transferable shares and active secondary trading.
    • London Coffee Houses (17th–18th Century): Informal trading in venues like Jonathan's Coffee House before formal stock exchanges; speculative excess led to bubbles and regulation.
    • Four Attributes Making the Modern Corporation Scalable (Slide 8): (1) Separate legal personality, (2) Perpetual succession, (3) Limited liability (early 19th-century breakthrough bounding investor downside), and (4) Transferable shares (investor exit without liquidating enterprise).
    • The Central Agency Question ("My Mind, Your Money" — Slide 9): Separation of ownership (capital providers) and control (managers) requires disclosure, governance, and anti-fraud rules.
  • Indian Market Evolution ("From Banyan Tree to Organised Exchange" — Slides 12–17):
    • 1830s: Early trading in loan securities & bank/EIC shares; 1861–65: American Civil War cotton boom & share mania in Bombay; 1875 / 1877: The Native Share & Stock Brokers' Association established (→ BSE); 1956: SCRA enacted.
    • Two Regulatory Philosophies (Slide 15): Merit / Control Orientation (state decides whether and on what terms capital is raised, e.g., India's war-time 1943–1947 Capital Issues Control regime under CCI) vs Disclosure / Market Orientation (pioneered by the US Securities Act of 1933 & Securities Exchange Act of 1934 after the 1929 crash; adopted by India in 1992 when CCI was repealed and the SEBI Act, 1992 gave SEBI statutory status).
    • 1996 Onwards Infrastructure Transformation (Slide 17): (1) Depositories Act, 1996, (2) Dematerialisation, (3) Screen-based trading, (4) Clearing corporation / CCP risk management, (5) Demutualisation (separation of ownership, management, and trading interests).
  • Market Infrastructure ("The Core Plumbing" — Slides 20–24 & 28):
    • Exchange: Trading venue, order matching, price discovery, market transparency.
    • Clearing Corporation: Clearing, counterparty-risk management, settlement guarantee, default management.
    • Depository: Electronic ownership record, dematerialisation, transfer, asset servicing.
    • Core Sequence: TRADE → CLEAR → SETTLE → RECORD OWNERSHIP.
    • Tech Progression (Slide 28): Paper → Demat → Screen Trading → Algorithms → Tokenisation → AI & Data. Four timeless questions: Who bears the risk? Who has the information? Who controls the infrastructure? Can the investor trust the market?

5.2 Statutory Definition of "Security" & Landmark Case Laws / Induction Cases

Statute / Case Law Core Legal Principle / Test Application in Induction Exam
Section 2(h) of SCRA, 1956 Inclusive definition: shares, scrips, stocks, bonds, debentures, derivatives (Sec 2(ac)), units of CIS (Sec 11AA SEBI Act), MF units, Security Receipts (SARFAESI Act 2002), and Govt securities. Primary statutory test in India for SEBI jurisdiction.
SEC v. W.J. Howey Co. (1946) 4-Prong Howey Test for "Investment Contract": (1) Investment of money, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with a reasonable expectation of profits, (4) derived primarily/solely from the efforts of others. Citrus grove land sales + service contracts held to be securities; applied to novel schemes/tokens.
United Housing Foundation v. Forman (1975) Co-op housing shares bought strictly to acquire a personal place to live (consumption, not profit) are NOT securities despite being called "stock". Economic reality ("substance over form") governs over the label "stock".
Landreth Timber Co. v. Landreth (1985) Traditional corporate stock possessing standard stock attributes (dividends, voting, negotiability, appreciation) is per se a security without needing the Howey test. Distinguishes commercial stock from co-op housing shares in Forman.
SEC v. C.M. Joiner Leasing Corp. (1943) Small oil leasehold parcels marketed alongside the promoter's promise to drill test wells constituted an investment contract (security). Promotional economic inducement turns leasehold sales into securities.
SEC v. Edwards (2004) & Munchee Inc. (2017) Edwards: A scheme promising a fixed rate of return (payphone leaseback) still satisfies Howey.
Munchee: Utility ICO tokens marketed with secondary-market appreciation promises from company efforts are securities.		 "Profits" include fixed returns; utility label does not exempt crypto tokens.
Induction Case Studies (Case-1 & Case-2):
• Case-1 (SG Software Ltd. — "Stock Generation" Virtual Stock Exchange Game): Promoters Ashok Aggarwal & Ramesh Gupta promised "double your money each month" on virtual shares to 10,000+ subscribers. Satisfies the Howey Test (US Sec 2(1)) and constitutes an unregistered Collective Investment Scheme (CIS under Sec 11AA of SEBI Act / Sec 2(h) SCRA) + fraudulent Ponzi scheme violating Section 12A of the SEBI Act, 1992 read with Regulations 3, 4 & 5 of SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003 (and US Sec 10(b) & Rule 10b-5).
• Case-2 ("Loan for Everyone" — P2P Fractional Digital Promissory Notes): Issuing fractionalized digital promissory notes across pooled lenders and launching a secondary marketplace crosses the line from bilateral P2P lending (under RBI NBFC-P2P Master Directions) into marketable debt securities / investment contracts under SCRA Sec 2(h) and US Securities Act Sec 2(1), requiring SEBI/SEC registration and prospectus disclosures.

6.1 Legal Structure, SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 & Two Sponsor Routes

  • Core Architecture ("A Pool, Not a Promise" — Slides 2–6): Constituted as a Trust (Indian Trusts Act, 1882) acting as a pure pass-through vehicle.
    • Unit (Reg 2(1)(ccc), was 2(1)(z) in 1996): "The interest of the unit holders in a scheme representing one undivided share in the assets of a scheme."
    • Mutual Fund (Reg 2(1)(gg), was 2(1)(q) in 1996): Explicitly lists securities, money market instruments, gold/gold-related, and silver/silver-related instruments.
    • Why Unitholders Have No Voting Rights in the AMC — Compensated by 3 Protections (Slides 5 & 22–23): (1) Trustees as surrogate principal (at least 2/3rd independent trustees under an independent chairperson per Reg 11), (2) Unconditional exit at daily NAV (Reg 43) and no-load exit if fundamental attributes change (Reg 22), and (3) Simple majority unitholder consent required before winding up a scheme (Reg 36).
    • Bank vs MF Balance Sheet (Slide 6 — Kotak Bank vs Kotak Flexicap FY26): Bank is leveraged $5.8 imes$ with DICGC ₹5L insurance; MF scheme has zero deposits, zero fixed assets, cannot grant loans (Reg 39), and can borrow at most 20% of net assets for up to 6 months strictly for redemptions/cash payouts (Reg 42). A SEBI-registered Custodian holds the securities (Reg 13).
  • SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 — Landmark Changes:
    • Notified: 14 January 2026 | In Force From: 1 April 2026 (replacing the 1996 Regulations).
    • Complete Deletion of Guaranteed Return & Capital Protection Schemes (Slide 2): Old Regulations 38 and 38A of the 1996 Regulations have been COMPLETELY REMOVED (following the June 2023 Board Memorandum).
Eligibility Parameter (Reg 5, Slide 19) Route 1: Track Record Test (Main Route) Route 2: Capital & People Test (Alternate Route)
Financial Services Experience & Profit 5 years in financial services + Net profit in all 5 years (averaging $\ge$ ₹10 Crore/yr) + positive net worth. No prior experience or profitability track record required on the sponsor.
AMC Net Worth & Lock-in At least ₹50 Crore, maintained continuously. At least ₹150 Crore at registration (locked in for 5 years); ₹100 Crore maintained until 5 consecutive profitable years.
Key Personnel & 3 New Openings Standard fit & proper norms. CEO, COO, CRO, CCO, CIO must have $\ge$ 30 years combined experience.
Plus: (1) PE as Sponsor (5 yrs + $\ge$ ₹5,000 Cr managed), (2) Self-Sponsored AMC (no shareholder $>10\%$, $\ge 2/3$ IDs), (3) MF Lite (passive-only AMCs).

7.1 Derivatives Fundamentals, Jurisdiction & 5-Step CCP Risk Plumbing

  • Statutory Definitions (SCRA, 1956 — Slide 18): Section 2(ac) defines "derivative"; Section 2(d) defines "option in securities" (includes teji, mandi, teji-mandi, galli, put, call). (Note: A bank fixed deposit is NOT a derivative — Slide 2).
  • Four Building Blocks & Payoffs (Slides 5 & 8–9):
    • Forwards (OTC) & Futures (Exchange-traded): Symmetric, linear payoff; both sides obligated.
    • Options: Asymmetric (kinked) payoff — Buyer pays upfront Premium for the right (max loss = Premium); Writer (Seller) receives Premium and bears unlimited downside obligation, so only the Option Writer posts margin! $ ext{Option Premium} = ext{Intrinsic Value} + ext{Time Value}$. All exchange-traded options in India are European style.
    • Settlement at Expiry (Slide 9): Physically Settled for Individual Stocks; Cash Settled for Indices and Currency.
  • Four Hedger Cases from Slide 7: (A) Exporter with $2M receivable in 90 days → Sell USD/INR Futures/Forwards or Buy USD Put; (B) Airline buying jet fuel monthly → Buy Crude/Fuel Futures or Call; (C) Equity Fund holding diversified portfolio expecting weak quarter → Sell Nifty Index Futures or Buy Nifty Put; (D) Infrastructure borrower with ₹500 Cr floating-rate loan → Pay-Fixed, Receive-Floating Interest Rate Swap (IRS).
  • SEBI vs RBI Jurisdiction (Slide 12): SEBI regulates Exchange-traded Equity/Index F&O, Commodity F&O (post-2015 FMC merger), Exchange Currency Derivatives, and Listed IRDs. RBI sets the FX framework and regulates OTC Rupee Interest Rate Derivatives (OIS), OTC FX forwards/swaps, and Credit Default Swaps (CDS).
  • 5-Step CCP Risk Plumbing & Default Waterfall (Slide 13–14): (1) Order match on exchange → (2) CCP Novation → (3) Upfront VaR-based Initial Margin + ELM → (4) Daily MTM settlement → (5) Default Waterfall Order: Defaulting Member's Margin → Defaulting Member's Deposits → Settlement Guarantee Fund (SGF) → Clearing Corporation's Own Capital. Worked Leverage Math (Slide 14): ₹15,00,000 notional on 12% margin (₹1,80,000) → a 2% adverse index move causes a ₹30,000 loss = -16.7% of margin in 1 day!
